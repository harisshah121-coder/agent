## Findings

### F1. Needle grippers (the industrial default for air-permeable textiles)

Needle grippers push angled needle pairs (typically 30° plunge angle) into the top ply and hold by the crossed-needle lock; the stroke sets the penetration depth and is the only control over whether the second ply is also caught. Vendors position them for composite preforms, fleece, nonwovens and foams; Schmalz lists "primary textiles" first for the SNG-V family. [S1]

Knit-damage and penetration-setting evidence:
- Needle grippers "can also leave permanent puncture marks in the material structure... it depends on whether these defects in the structure are acceptable" (textile network, 2020, on grippers for textile substrates; note it names woven and knitted fabrics as a use case). [S2] https://textile-network.com/en/Technical-Textiles/Technologien/Mobile-robots-and-grippers-for-textile-substrates
- Composite-layer handling review (2020): needle grippers give high holding force but penetration "can result in damage and displacement of the fibers"; Seliger's holding-force model shows force rising with needle count and falling penetration angle, piercing angle 20–40° recommended; needle grippers "physically hold all layers", so they are poor single-ply separators from a stack, whereas vacuum holds the top layer most firmly. [S3] https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/20550340.2020.1750329
- Systematic review of gripper technologies for fabric parts (2024, PMC): intrusive mechanisms (needles, brushes) "provide strong grip and ply separation but risk damaging surfaces, mitigated slightly by thin needles or soft grippers"; cites Ku et al.'s soft gripper with micro-needles and integrated air-pressure sensors for garment manufacturing. [S4] https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11617252
- No published test of needle marks on cotton single jersey (160–200 g/m²) was found; this must be measured on our fabric (see Open questions). Design implication: for a single ply on a vacuum bed there is no ply below, so stroke only needs to exceed fabric thickness (≈0.5–0.9 mm for jersey, est.) plus the needle crossing length; the SNG-M (3 mm fixed) and FIPA GR04.700 (0–2 mm) are the right size class, the SNG-V 7–18 mm strokes are oversized for single jersey unless dialled down.

### F2. Vacuum: foam and flat area grippers, cobot vacuum grippers, Bernoulli

Area/foam grippers hold through many small cells with check valves (SVK) or flow resistors (SW), so an uncovered or leaky cell does not kill the vacuum; porous knits need the SVK/check-valve variants and high-flow generation. Datasheets say porous materials (fabrics, foams) reach a lower vacuum than the nominal range. [S5][S6]
