## Findings

Scope note: all cutters below are compared at the cell's working width. The white paper's product is a heavyweight (280 gsm) cotton single-jersey T-shirt [131]; typical open-width jersey rolls are 60–72 in (1.5–1.8 m) wide, so the cutter needs a **1.8 m (70 in) class** working width with conveyor. Zünd calls this the "L" size (1800 mm with material transport) [2]; Eastman offers 72 in and 78 in [33]; Bullmer offers 1800 mm [42]; Kuris EDGE offers 1.60 / 1.75 m [59][60].

### A. Roll handling (ST01): unwind / let-off, cradles, A-frames, loading aids

Key facts from vendors:
- Zünd's own roll-handling range (for G3/S3/D3) comprises passive universal roll-off, passive and **active (motor-driven) shaft roll-off** (rewinds slack at the end of unwinding), the **Center Winder** (rolls up to **100 kg / 220 lb**, driven unwind *and* rewind, "consistent feeding tension ... even challenging textiles advanced without creases") and the **Cradle Feeder 100** (no shaft; damaged cores accepted; rapid rewind) [1][4][5]. Sizes/weights per cutter size are in the roll-off overview PDF [5] (not retrieved; request). Price: not published; request quote.
- Bullmer's apparel configuration uses the **AWV-A unwinding unit with center drive and edge alignment**, optionally with the **Transroll-P** roll magazine for automatic roll change [41]. A used Bullmer **Single-Feeder** (rolls ≤1800 mm wide, ≤70 kg, ≤400 mm dia., roll motor + dancer bar for tension-free roll-off, edge control, cones) was listed by Bullmer at **€8,950 net** [44] — the only hard price found for a cutter-grade knit feeder.
- Eastman sells the **Power Cradle** ("feeding ... from a cradle, in a relaxed or tensionless state with precise edge control") and **Power Feed** 1/2/3-roll driven stands, roll stands, and the A-Frame manual feeder (78 in standard; 8 rolls; 250 lb per bar; 16 in max roll dia.; widths 60–132 in) [37][38][66]. Prices not published (request-a-quote shop) [38].
- Kongsberg's **Motorized Roll Feeder** (sign/graphics heritage but the best-documented spec set): max roll width 3210 mm, max dia. 470 mm, max **300 kg**, dancer-loop control with adjustable weights, **edge correction on the shaft up to ±50 mm**, spreader (brush) roll, 23 m/min, docks to the table with magnets, 220 V/50 Hz or 110 V/60 Hz [64]. Price not published.
- Caron **Vegas-DUO** (Italian unwinder sold with the FASTSEWN system): expansion shaft or **dual motorized roller** unwinding, 100–200 kg rolls, useful widths 180–320 cm, roll dia. 35/40 cm, "electronic control of fabric tension, synchronization with electronic dancer bar or material loop", automatic alignment, handles 170–330 gsm and "stretchy fabrics" [63]. Price not published.
- Other cradle unwinders listed on DirectIndustry: Cutting Trading International **Cradle K3** (narrow- or broad-belt driven), Sodifa **ESCA 528 BT**, Handsaeme A-frames with pneumatic shaft, drive unit and edge control [66].
- Pathfinder's EasyLoader synchronises the feed conveyor to the cutter and claims this "eliminates the traditional dancer bar" for single-layer delivery without stretch [125] — relevant design precedent for our own feed path (synchronised driven nip/conveyor instead of a free dancer).

**Tension control components (for a self-built let-off).** Dancer-arm unwind control is the standard, forgiving method for low-tension webs; the controller trims brake/motor torque to hold the dancer at set-point, and a diameter sensor can compensate inertia across the roll [67]. Mitsubishi's roll-to-roll guide documents the servo-driven unwind + dancer-position loop and the "tension generated by dancer roll weight" architecture that suits knits (tension set by a light dancer mass or low air-cylinder pressure, not by torque) [68]. Double E's controller (24 V DC, dancer or load-cell input, 0–10 V/4–20 mA out, Modbus RS232) is a representative off-the-shelf closed-loop unit [69]. A knit-safe target is a few N across the web: the white paper requires "low tension ... not uncontrolled slack" [131]; no vendor publishes a knit tension number — must be measured (see Open questions).

### A3. Knit relaxation (the "24-hour practice") and relaxing machines

- Practice: knitted fabric must relax 24–48 h before cutting (textile-engineering references: 24–48 h [84]; 100% cotton jersey 180 gsm shrinks 5–7 % length × 3–5 % width if cut unrelaxed; cotton/5 % spandex needs 48–72 h [84]). Oshima (relaxing-machine maker) states 12–24 h natural relaxation, or **6–8 h with steam-assisted relaxing** [78][79]. Svegea and Suntech sell air-flotation relaxers that claim to "eliminate at least 24 h rest" (vendor claim, not independently verified) [81][83].
- The white paper already assumes relaxation may be needed and that "a fast in-cell feeder cannot be assumed to replace all conditioning" [131] — consistent with these sources. Implication for the cell: relaxation is an **offline batch step** (roll in → plaited/relaxed → re-rolled) performed by the operator the day before; it does not belong inside the 12-hour takt.

### C. Single-ply knife cutters (ST02)

### D. Vacuum hold-down: blowers, zoning, underlay, sealing porous knits

**What the OEMs do.** Zünd: "energy-efficient vacuum turbines", vacuum area **zoned across the width, individually switchable** [1]; Bullmer: "automatic vacuum chamber sectioning", pump sized 4–7.5 kW for textiles and "it is also possible to save energy costs by using smaller vacuum pumps" because the electric rotary blade and notch tool need no plastic foil [41]; Eastman C125/C135: 7.5 kW (10 HP) VFD side-channel blower *inside the frame*, 6,000 cfm, "millions of holes perforated in a random pattern" in the belt, "often capable of cutting multiple layers **without plastic overlay** (material dependent)" [33][35]; Gerber DCS 2500: "no paper underlay or plastic overlay required", pneumatic single/dual-zone vacuum shutoff, **reverse air for flotation of non-porous materials during spreading** [30]; Lectra Vector (multi-ply): "dynamic vacuum control" and paper/plastic roll goods "to achieve homogeneity of vacuum" [126] — i.e. the film overlay is a multi-ply practice; single-ply rotary/oscillating cutters generally run bare.
**Underlay.** Single-ply knife cutters run on a **felt conveyor belt** (2.5–4 mm; Chinese tables specify "4 mm felt", Novo sells 2.5/4.0 mm felt belts for Zünd/Kuris/Elitron) [49][122], Eastman uses cast urethane [33], Zünd's WKT wheel knife uses a PU underlay [1]. **Bristle (brush) beds** are the multi-ply straight-knife solution (Bullmer NextGen: "bristle conveyor ... cleaning run sucks out the bristle conveyor"; vacuum must drop before the bristle conveyor advances) [123] — not appropriate for single-ply oscillating/rotary blades, which need a continuous felt/PU surface.
**Porous knit leakage.** No vendor publishes a leakage number. Physics: a 163 gsm cotton single jersey has air permeability ~89 cm³/cm²/s at the standard 100 Pa test [113]; at the ~10–30 kPa a side-channel blower pulls, flow through 1.8 × 2.5 m of bare jersey is of the same order as the blower's open flow, which is why OEMs (a) zone the plenum so only the strip under the gantry is live, (b) use high-flow/low-vacuum side-channel blowers rather than high-vacuum pumps, (c) rely on felt resistance plus nested pieces covering most of the belt, and (d) for very open fabrics add a film overlay (multi-ply) — the white paper's extraction design must assume the vacuum pattern "may help on some materials but leak or lift unwanted layers on others" [131]. Sealing practice for the cell: cut with the felt saturated by the fabric lay, keep the un-covered belt area minimal (nest to the belt edges or mask), and size the blower by *flow at ~100–150 mbar*, not by ultimate vacuum.

### E. Blades (oscillating / drag / rotary) and blade life on cotton jersey

- Zünd blade system (catalog 09/2025 and 06/2026): drag blades for UCT (Z10 1.5 mm carbide 4.8 mm depth, Z11, Z16 0.63 mm pointed 7.4 mm, Z17 12 mm, Z104 flat 11 mm); **oscillating blades for EOT/EOT-250/POT (Z16, Z17, Z20–Z29, Z60 …)**; **decagonal rotary blades Z50/Z51/Z53 for DRT/PRT**; "overcut distorts contours — choose a blade with smaller overcut for low-margin cuts"; Zünd may refuse warranty for non-original blades [3][102]. Textile-recommended: Z16 ("blade for soft materials", EOT/POT), Z26 flat ("woven materials, textiles, leather, felt") [3][101].
- Prices: Zünd Z10 **301 DKK (~€40) list** (Zünd Store DK, 2026-10-21) [98]; Sollex Z16 carbide clone **802 SEK (listing "5 pcs"; ~€72)** [99]; Toro-tec Z16-compatible **€10.40**, Z17 €10.40, Type-6 HSS €1.10 [100]; Namicam Z10-compatible €17.99, Z26-compatible €12.99, **decagonal rotary blade 8 × 0.63 mm (22–60 mm dia.) €11.99** [101]; Summa OEM 36° tangential blades 5-pack US$265 [103].
- Blade life: **Lectra publishes "35 km average blade lifespan" for the Virga rotary blade** on garment fabrics [21][22] — the only vendor number found; Zünd/Bullmer publish none. At a T-shirt perimeter of ~4–5 m per garment (est., 4 panels + notches) 35 km ≈ 7,000–9,000 shirts per blade. Cotton is abrasive relative to synthetics; carbide (HM) blades are specified for life [3][99]. Treat Zünd DRT/EOT life on 280 gsm cotton jersey as **unknown — measure in a cutting trial** (Open questions).
- Blade choice for single jersey (vendor guidance): Zünd DRT driven rotary "clean, precise cuts even on very loose, coarsely knit materials" [8]; Bullmer apparel config uses the electric rotary knife + notch tool and states this removes the need for foil [41]; Timing Weiye's jersey configuration lists 25/32 mm round blades plus V/I notch [119]; MNT recommends conveyor + vacuum + controlled speed for stretch knits and uses the oscillating knife for near-zero kerf [118]. Consensus: **driven rotary (decagonal) blade for long contours; oscillating or notch tool for notches and small radii.**

### H. Fabric behaviour: single-ply cutting of cotton single jersey

- **Curl**: single jersey "will curl on the edges when they are cut and laid free" due to the unbalanced loop structure and yarn torque; curl depends on structure and yarn twist and "results in cutting, sewing and linking problems" [113]; a 2024 study measured curling of 42–56 % on cotton/spandex knits [114]. Mitigations found in products: Kuris "fabric edge-blowing feature against curling" [62]; vacuum hold-down keeps edges flat *while on the belt*; curl reappears on release, which is why the white paper's ST03 pick strategy must grip within the first seconds after vacuum release or pick under vacuum.
- **Dimensional instability**: cotton single jersey relaxes/shrinks 5–7 % × 3–5 % if cut unrelaxed [84]; Scott (2026) characterises dimensional, growth and stretch properties of cotton/merino single jersey and seams with an ASTM D2594-based method — the right test protocol for the material profile (M05) [115].
- **Accuracy**: machine accuracies are ±0.1 mm-class (Zünd ±0.1 mm/m, Bullmer repeat 0.1 mm, Autometrix ±0.51 mm, Eastman ±0.4 mm) [2][40][54][36]; **fabric-side error dominates**: textbook data for knitted garments report knife-force shifts of up to 2 mm at cut starts and end-of-ply allowances of 10–20 mm because of instability [116]; manual single-ply cutting tolerance in the NZ apparel standard is ±1.5 mm [117]. A realistic target for a 280 gsm jersey on a vacuum felt belt with a rotary blade is **±1–2 mm on the panel after release** (est.; no published single-ply-on-knit accuracy study was found — verify by trial).
- **Blade**: rotary/decagonal driven blade for jersey (Zünd DRT, Bullmer electric rotary, Timing Weiye 25/32 mm round) with notch tool; oscillating 0.63 mm pointed blade (Z16 class) for tight radii [8][41][119]. Zünd's TechTex application overview explicitly lists "garment fabrics: cotton, polyester, velour …" and "stretch fabrics: elastane, spandex" as G3 materials [124].
